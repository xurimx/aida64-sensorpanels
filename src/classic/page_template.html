Before you import

  1. Check the sensors that depend on your PC.Network reads NIC1, storage reads drives C: and D:, and the case fans are Chassis #1 to #3. Change any of these in SensorPanel Manager if your hardware differs.
  2. CPU temperature reads CPU Package.If it stays at zero on your CPU, switch the dial's items to CPU Diode (TCPUDIO).
  3. FPS needs RivaTuner Statistics Server.The frame-rate dial reads RTSS, so it sits at 0 unless RTSS is running with your game.
  4. Each needle is two or three stacked gauges.AIDA64 gauges have only 16 frames, so every dial stacks gauges with overlapping ranges to get 26 or 41 needle positions. To change a dial's sensor, change all of its gauges plus its number. Hidden divider labels group them in the item list.
  5. Readings use Bahnschrift.It ships with Windows 10 and 11. Scale numbers and labels are drawn into the background, so they look the same on any PC.

OLED care

  • True black everywhere.The background is pure #000000, so those pixels stay off. There are no panels, gradients or glows.
  • Dim fixed markings, bright moving parts.Ticks, numbers and labels never change, so they're kept at roughly 35–75% brightness. The orange needles and cream readings move, so they spread wear across more pixels.
  • Let the screen do pixel shift.AIDA64 doesn't move the panel, so leave the monitor's own pixel shift, screen saver or panel refresh switched on.
  • Run it at a modest brightness.On an OLED the dim markings stay readable even at low brightness, and that's where burn-in risk is lowest.
